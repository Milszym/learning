Lesson 7 · Grammar

Present tenses for attitude

Why "You're always doing that" is a complaint, and "I'm seeing someone" is gossip.

Time ~12 min Win hear the feeling behind 5 present-tense choices Mission catch irritation, politeness and storytelling in dialogue

The big idea

At C1, the choice between I do and I'm doing is often about attitude, not time. The continuous can sound irritated, temporary or softer. The simple can make a story feel as if it's happening in front of you.1

Annoying habits

always / constantly / forever + present continuous

You're always taking my charger!

He's forever telling me how to load the dishwasher.

These are repeated, unplanned and often unwanted events.2

Behaviour right now

be being + adjective

She's friendly. (her character)

You're being ridiculous. (how you're acting now)3

State verbs, used as actions

love / see / have… + -ing = a new meaning

She's loving the CD you gave her. (enjoying it now)

Is she seeing anyone? (dating)4

We're having a meeting. (holding, not owning)3

Softening

I'm hoping… · I was hoping / wondering…

I was wondering if you could help.

Softer than I wonder if…. The past continuous is the most polite of all.5

Stories and jokes

present simple for events · continuous for background

So this guy walks into a bar…

I'm standing at the bus stop and this woman comes up to me…6

Polish shortcut

Where Polish says wiecznie gubisz klucze or ciągle mi przerywasz, English uses the continuous: You're forever losing your keys, You're always interrupting me. You always interrupt me is also correct, but it sounds more like a plain fact about a habit.

I'm loving it is fine in casual speech, but it's informal. Cambridge frames it as love used as an action (enjoying something right now), not as a general preference.3

Listen for it

Real-world task · next episode you watch

Sitcoms and family dramas are full of this. Listen for a complaint with always / constantly / forever + -ing, and for someone telling a story in the present (So I'm sitting there, and he goes…). Ask yourself: what's the attitude? Irritated, polite, or telling a story? Bring your best line back to the chat.
