Honey never spoils

Bees build a food that microbes simply can't live in.

Time ~2 min Win three reasons honey lasts, ready to explain Bonus 1 C1 word

Guess firstIn a properly sealed jar, how long does honey stay safe to eat: about two years, about twenty years, or practically for ever? Decide before you scroll.

Sealed properly, honey essentially never spoils: it's too dry, too acidic and too sugary for microbes.

17–18%water in ripe honey (nectar: 60–80%)
pH ≈ 4acidic, from gluconic acid
H₂O₂hydrogen peroxide, made by a bee enzyme

Why it works: Bees fan nectar until only about 17–18% water is left, so the sugar pulls water out of any microbe that lands in it.1 A bee enzyme, glucose oxidase, adds gluconic acid and a little hydrogen peroxide.2 The catch is the seal: open honey soaks up moisture from humid air and can ferment. The popular "edible honey from pharaohs' tombs" story is often repeated but poorly documented.

Retell it · say it out loud once
  1. Hook: "Why is honey the one food in your kitchen that never goes off?"
  2. Detail: "Bees dry nectar from about 70% water down to 17%, then add acid and a dash of hydrogen peroxide."
  3. Kicker: "Microbes don't die of hunger in honey. They die of thirst."
