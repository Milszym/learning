Robot means forced labour

A Czech play gave the world its word for machines. Polish speakers already know the root.

Time ~2 min Win a word story with a Polish twist Bonus 1 C1 word

Guess firstThe word "robot" comes from a Czech play of 1920. What did its Czech root originally mean? Decide before you scroll.

"Robot" comes from Czech robota, the forced labour serfs owed their lords, and it was Karel Čapek's brother Josef who suggested it.

Why it works: Čapek first wanted to call the artificial workers in his play R.U.R. "labori", from Latin labor; his brother Josef, a painter, suggested "roboti".1 In Czech, robota meant corvée, the unpaid work serfs owed a landlord (abolished in 1848), and so drudgery.2 Polish robota, from the same Slavic root, simply means "work".3 Twist: Čapek's robots were made of synthetic flesh, not metal.1

Retell it · say it out loud once
  1. Hook: "Do you know which language gave English the word 'robot'?"
  2. Detail: "Karel Čapek's 1920 play R.U.R. His brother Josef suggested 'roboti', from robota: the forced labour of serfs."
  3. Kicker: "A Pole hears robota and thinks 'work'. A Czech in 1920 heard 'serfdom'."

Hard, boring work.4 Čapek's robots were made for drudgery, the very meaning of robota.
