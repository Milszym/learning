The birthday paradox

Two football teams plus the referee: enough for a coin-flip chance of a shared birthday.

Time ~2 min Win a bet you'll usually win at parties Bonus 1 C1 word

Guess firstHow many people do you need in a room for a better-than-even chance that two share a birthday? Decide before you scroll.

With just 23 people, the chance that two share a birthday is already about 50.7%.

23people in the room
253possible pairs to compare
50.7%chance of a shared birthday

Why it feels wrong: We picture ourselves: the chance that one of 22 others shares my birthday is only about 6%. But any pair counts, and 23 people make 253 pairs; with 70 people the chance hits 99.9%.1 The maths assumes 365 equally likely birthdays and ignores leap years. Real births are slightly uneven, which only raises the odds, and real data still give 23.2

Retell it · say it out loud once
  1. Hook: "How many people do you need before two probably share a birthday?"
  2. Numbers: "Just 23. That's 253 pairs and a 50.7% chance. With 70 people, it's 99.9%."
  3. Kicker: "It's not about your birthday. It's about any two people, and pairs pile up fast."

counterintuitiveadjective

Not happening in the way you would expect.3 It's counterintuitive, but two football teams and a referee are enough for an even chance of a shared birthday.
