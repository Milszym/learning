Glass is not a liquid

Old church windows really are thicker at the bottom. Gravity didn't do it.

Time ~2 min Win a classroom myth you can take apart Bonus 1 C1 word

Guess firstWhy are some medieval windows thicker at the bottom? Decide before you scroll.

The panes were made uneven and usually fitted thick edge down; the glass has not flowed.

Why it feels wrong: Glass has a disordered, liquid-like structure, so "slow flow" sounds plausible. But medieval panes were spun from a blown disc (the crown process), which came out uneven, and glaziers tended to put the heavy edge at the bottom.3 A 2017 study of Westminster Abbey glass from 1268 found it flows at most about 1 nanometre per billion years.12

Retell it · say it out loud once
  1. Hook: "Why are old cathedral windows thicker at the bottom? Most people say glass is a slow liquid."
  2. Numbers: "Westminster Abbey glass from 1268 flows at most one nanometre per billion years. Over the whole age of the universe, about fourteen."
  3. Kicker: "The sag was there on day one: blame the glassmaker, not gravity."
