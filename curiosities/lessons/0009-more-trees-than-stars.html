More trees than stars

The galaxy looks endless. Your planet's forests are bigger, by count.

Time ~2 min Win a big-number comparison you'll never forget Bonus 1 C1 word

Guess firstWhich is bigger: the number of trees on Earth, or the number of stars in the Milky Way? Decide before you scroll.

Earth has about 3 trillion trees, far more than the 100–400 billion stars in the Milky Way.

Why it feels wrong: Stars feel countless; trees feel countable. In 2015, Crowther and colleagues combined tree counts from over 400,000 forest plots with satellite maps and got 3.04 trillion trees (trunks over 10 cm wide at chest height).1 The old estimate, from satellites alone, was about 400 billion.2 NASA puts the Milky Way at 100–400 billion stars: a rough range, but even the top figure loses.3

Retell it · say it out loud once
  1. Hook: "Are there more trees on Earth or stars in our galaxy?"
  2. Numbers: "About 3 trillion trees against 100 to 400 billion stars. That's roughly 8 to 30 trees for every star."
  3. Kicker: "Before 2015 we guessed 400 billion trees: we'd undercounted our own planet by a factor of seven and a half."

staggeringadjective

Very shocking and surprising, especially because extremely large.4 Earth has a staggering 3 trillion trees, about 400 for every person.
