Lesson 2 · Grammar

Past tense, present meaning

Why a native speaker says "It's time we went" about right now.

Time ~12 min Win decode and use the unreal past after 4 triggers Mission catch what characters wish, regret and resent

The big idea

In English, a past form doesn't always mean past time. Sometimes it means not real: wished for, imagined, or overdue. The British Council puts it simply: the tense shifts back to show distance from reality.1 You already do this in the second conditional (If I had more time…). The same shift happens after a few other trigger phrases.

The shift

Unreal now → past simple · I wish I knew.
Unreal past → past perfect · I wish I had known.

Polish shortcut

Polish does something similar after żeby: Najwyższy czas, żebyśmy poszli → It's high time we went; Wolałbym, żebyś nie mówił → I'd rather you didn't tell. Trust that instinct here. One warning: Polish uses żeby for real wishes too (Chcę, żebyś przyszedł), but English uses an infinitive for those: I want you to come, never I want you came.

The four triggers

wish / if only

+ past simple · past perfect · would

I wish I knew what she sees in him. (now)

If only we'd known. (past regret)

I wish you'd stop that! (annoyance at someone else's behaviour)

It's (high / about) time

+ subject + past simple

It's high time we went home. It should already be happening.

No subject? Use the infinitive: It's time to go.

would rather

+ other subject + past simple

I'd rather you didn't tell Mum.

Same subject? Use the bare infinitive: I'd rather stay in.2

as if / as though

+ past for an unreal comparison

He talks to me as if I were five. (I'm not.)

She looked as though she'd seen a ghost.3

was or were? After I / he / she / it, were is the traditional, more formal choice (as if I were five). Was is common in everyday speech. You'll hear both on screen.3
