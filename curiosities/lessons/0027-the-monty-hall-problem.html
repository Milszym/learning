The Monty Hall problem

Three doors, one car, and a puzzle that fooled a thousand PhDs.

Time ~2 min Win a puzzle to try on any friend Bonus 1 C1 word

Guess firstA car is behind one of three doors, goats behind the others. You pick a door. The host, who knows where the car is, opens another door to show a goat. Should you switch? Decide before you scroll.

Switching wins 2 times out of 3; sticking wins only 1 in 3.

Why it works: Your first pick is right only 1 time in 3, and the host's reveal can't change that. Because he knows where the car is and always opens a goat door, the other closed door gets the remaining 2/3.2 The catch: it holds only if he always reveals a goat and offers the switch.1 When Marilyn vos Savant said this in Parade in 1990, nearly 1,000 PhDs were among those who wrote in to disagree.1

Retell it · say it out loud once
  1. Hook: "Three doors, a car behind one. You pick, the host opens a goat door. Would you switch?"
  2. Numbers: "Stick wins 1 in 3, switch wins 2 in 3. About 10,000 letters told vos Savant she was wrong."
  3. Kicker: "Even the great mathematician Paul Erdős only believed it after seeing a computer simulation."
