The first computer bug

A real moth, a famous logbook, and a word that was already 70 years old.

Time ~2 min Win a myth you can gently correct Bonus 1 C1 word

Guess firstWhen did people first call a technical fault a "bug"? Decide before you scroll.

In 1947 a real moth was taped into a computer logbook, but engineers had called faults "bugs" since at least the 1870s.

Why it feels wrong: the moth story is true, just not the origin. On 9 September 1947, operators of Harvard's Mark II found a moth stuck in a relay and taped it in the log: "First actual case of bug being found."1 That note is a joke: they already knew the word. Edison wrote of "bugs" in 1878.2 Grace Hopper made the story famous, though she probably didn't find the moth.3

Retell it · say it out loud once
  1. Hook: "Do you know why programming errors are called bugs?"
  2. Numbers: "In 1947, Harvard engineers found a real moth in their computer and taped it into the logbook. But Edison was already calling faults 'bugs' in 1878."
  3. Kicker: "The note said 'first actual case of bug being found': the joke only works if the word already existed."

apocryphaladjective

An apocryphal story is probably not true, although it is often told and believed.4 The moth is real, but the idea that it gave us the word "bug" is apocryphal.
