# Curiosities Resources

## Knowledge

- [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/)
  Non-profit, editor-reviewed history encyclopedia with sources. Use for: history curiosities and readable primary-source recommendations.
- [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/)
  Good for dates and numbers with citations; follow its references for the real source. Use for: cross-checking figures, finding the underlying papers.
- [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/)
  Primary source for spaceflight and astronomy facts. Use for: space curiosities.
- [Wait But Why: Horizontal History](https://waitbutwhy.com/2016/01/horizontal-history.html)
  Tim Urban on seeing who lived at the same time. Use for: "time is weirder than you think" curiosities.
- [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/) and [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/)
  Fact-checking and data-backed perspective. Use for: checking viral "fun facts" before teaching them.
- [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/)
  Use for: verifying the "Words to steal" definitions.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/AskHistorians](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskHistorians/)
  Strictly moderated; answers must come from experts, with sources. Use for: "is this history fact really true?"
- [r/askscience](https://www.reddit.com/r/askscience/)
  Panel of verified scientists. Use for: follow-up "but why?" questions on science curiosities.
- Real life: retell one curiosity a week to a friend or colleague. Their follow-up questions are the real test.
