Lesson 5 · Vocabulary

Phrasal verbs in dialogue

Ten verbs that carry the drama in almost every row, reunion and wind-up on screen.

Time ~12 min Win use 10 conversational phrasal verbs in the right word order Mission follow who's annoyed, who's forgiven and who's been let down

The big idea

In real conversation people rarely say tolerate, disappoint or reconcile. They say put up with, let someone down and make up. The meaning often can't be guessed from the parts, so learn each one as a whole.2 Then learn where the object goes, because a pronoun in the wrong place sounds instantly foreign.1

The ten verbs

Relationships

fall out with · make up · let someone down

He fell out with his dad years ago. (argued and stopped being friends)3

They screamed at each other, then kissed and made up. (forgave each other; UK also make it up)4

You promised. Don't let me down. (disappoint me)5

Tolerating and annoying

put up with · wind someone up · get away with

I don't know why she puts up with him. (accepts something unpleasant)6

He knows exactly how to wind me up. (annoy me) · Are you winding me up? (joking with a lie). UK informal.7

He cheats and always gets away with it. (avoids punishment)8

Ideas, topics and impressions

come up with · bring up · pick up on · come across as

She's come up with a plan. (thought of) · Don't bring up money. (start talking about)910

Nobody picked up on the lie. (noticed what others missed)11

He comes across as arrogant. (gives that impression)12

Where does the object go?

pronoun in the middle · preposition keeps it together

Separable: bring it up, let her down, wind him up. A pronoun must go in the middle: never bring up it.1

With a preposition: put up with it, come up with it, pick up on it. Never split these.1

No object: We made up.

Polish shortcut

Are you winding me up? is close to Wkręcasz mnie?: "you're telling me something false as a joke." But the more common British sense is to annoy: He really winds me up ≈ Strasznie mnie wkurza. Context tells you which one it is.
