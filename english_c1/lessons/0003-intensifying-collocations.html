Lesson 3 · Vocabulary

Intensifying collocations

Why natives say "bitterly disappointed" and "absolutely exhausted", and never "very exhausted".

Time ~12 min Win pick the right "very" word for 8 key adjectives and verbs Mission hear how strongly a character really feels

The big idea

English has lots of words that mean "very", but each has favourite partners. Very is safe with ordinary adjectives, yet natives reach for fixed pairs: you're bitterly disappointed, not highly disappointed. These pairs are collocations, just like rave reviews from Lesson 1. There's also one real grammar rule: extreme or absolute adjectives don't normally take very.12

Polish shortcut and trap

Several pairs match Polish word for word: głęboko żałuję → deeply regret, w pełni świadomy → fully aware, wysoce nieprawdopodobne → highly unlikely. The trap: bardzo goes with almost anything (bardzo wyczerpany), but very doesn't. Say absolutely exhausted or really exhausted.

The pairs to know

Feelings

bitterly disappointed · deeply regret

He was bitterly disappointed not to get the part.3

I deeply regret what I said.4

How likely, how sure

highly unlikely · strongly suspect · fully aware

It's highly unlikely he'll call back.5

I strongly suspect she knew all along.6

I'm fully aware of the risks, thanks.7

Judging a situation

utterly ridiculous · painfully obvious · perfectly normal

The whole thing was utterly ridiculous. (utterly = completely; often with negative words)8

It was painfully obvious they had nothing in common. (clear, and awkward)9

It's perfectly normal to feel nervous. (reassuring)10

very vs absolutely

very + gradable · absolutely + extreme

very tired · very angry · very good

absolutely exhausted · absolutely furious · absolutely brilliant

Really works with both: really tired, really exhausted.12

Not a hard wall. You will occasionally hear very exhausted in casual speech, but the grammar books call it unusual. Don't copy it; absolutely or really always sounds right.

Listen for it

Real-world task · next episode you watch

Listen for any "very" word that isn't very: absolutely, utterly, bitterly, deeply, perfectly, painfully. Each time, note the word that follows it. In argument or break-up scenes they come thick and fast. With Language Reactor, search the subtitle panel for "-ly". Bring back one pair that's new to you and we'll check it.
