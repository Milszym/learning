OK was a joke

The world's handiest word started as a deliberate misspelling.

Time ~2 min Win the real origin story of "OK" Bonus 1 C1 word

Guess firstWhere does the word "OK" come from: a Choctaw word, a Civil War code, or a joke in a Boston newspaper? Decide before you scroll.

"OK" first appeared in a Boston newspaper in 1839 as a joke: "o.k." for "oll korrect", a misspelling of "all correct".

Why it works: In the late 1830s, Boston papers had a craze for jokey abbreviations of misspelt phrases, such as OW ("oll wright") and GT ("gone to Texas").1 Most died out. OK survived because in 1840 Martin Van Buren's supporters reused it for his nickname, "Old Kinderhook".2 The linguist Allen Walker Read dug up the evidence in 1963–64; rival stories (a Choctaw word, for example) have no such paper trail.4

ubiquitousadjective

Seeming to be everywhere; found or existing everywhere.5 "OK" is now so ubiquitous that you hear it in Polish, Japanese and almost every language in between.
