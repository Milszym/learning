Lesson 10 · Vocabulary

Idioms in conversation

Ten idioms characters use when they're fed up, dodging the truth, or wrapping things up.

Time ~12 min Win decode and use 10 everyday spoken idioms Mission follow arguments, teasing and office drama

The big idea

You already know idioms like spill the beans. The ones below are just as common in dialogue, and most are informal. It's easier to learn them by what the speaker is doing (losing patience, avoiding a topic, judging timing) than one by one. That's also how you'll guess an unfamiliar one from context: ask what job it's doing in the scene.

Losing patience

the last straw · be (skating) on thin ice · take the mickey

His lying was the last straw. (also the final straw: the latest problem that makes it all unacceptable)1

You're on thin ice, mate. (in a risky position, close to real trouble)2

Are you taking the mickey? (UK, informal: making fun of me? Also take the mick)3

Honesty and avoidance

the elephant in the room · beat about / around the bush

Can we talk about the elephant in the room? (an obvious problem nobody wants to mention)1

Stop beating about the bush. (avoiding the important point; around is used in the UK and US, about only in the UK)1

Timing

jump the gun · call it a day

Let's not jump the gun. (act too soon, without thinking)1

I'm shattered. Let's call it a day. (informal: stop for now; also retire)4

Attitude and judgement

cut sb some slack · not my cup of tea · a blessing in disguise

Cut him some slack, his wife just had a baby. (informal: judge less harshly)1

Opera's not really my cup of tea. (not the kind of thing I like)1

Losing that job was a blessing in disguise. (bad at first, good in the end)1

Polish shortcut

Several have close Polish twins: the last straw ≈ kropla, która przelała czarę; beat about the bush ≈ owijać w bawełnę; not my cup of tea ≈ nie moja bajka; take the mickey ≈ nabijać się z kogoś. Trap: the last straw is not ostatnia deska ratunku. That's a last resort.

Listen for it

Real-world task · next episode you watch

British comedy-dramas and workplace series are full of these. Catch one idiom from each group: a line where someone loses patience, one where they avoid (or demand) the truth, and one about timing. For each, ask: is the speaker annoyed, joking or kind? With Language Reactor, search the subtitles for straw, bush or mickey. Bring your best line back to the chat.
