Lesson 9 · Grammar

Advanced passives

From the newsreader's "He is believed to have fled" to the pub's "He got sacked".

Time ~12 min Win decode 5 passive patterns in news and chat Mission follow crime dramas, documentaries and gossip

The big idea

The passive puts the focus on what happened, not on who did it, often because the doer is unknown or unimportant.2 On screen it has two very different voices. News reports and crime dramas use the reporting passive to pass on what "people" believe without naming anyone. Casual speech uses get: got sacked, got caught, got stuck.1

Polish shortcut

Mówi się, że… → It is said that…; Podobno zbił fortunę → He's said to have made a fortune. One trap: in Polish you'd say Obcięłam włosy after visiting the hairdresser, but in English I've cut my hair suggests you did it yourself. Use I've had/got my hair cut.3

Five patterns

It is said / thought / believed that…

It + be + reporting verb (past participle) + that-clause

It's thought that the fire was started deliberately.

It is believed that the gang had inside help.1

Someone is said to (have)…

subject + be said/thought/believed + to do · to have done

She's said to be furious. (now)

The suspect is believed to have fled the country. (earlier)

He's said to have made a fortune.1

The get-passive

get + past participle · informal speech, often bad news

He got sacked on Friday. (mainly BrE; US usually got fired)

We got stuck in traffic. · Don't get caught!4

have / get something done

have/get + object + past participle

I'm getting my hair cut on Saturday. (a service; get is less formal)

He had his car stolen. (a bad experience he didn't cause)3

Passive -ing

being + past participle (after hate, love, can't stand, prepositions…)

I hate being told what to do.

She couldn't stand being kept waiting.5

Which infinitive? to be / to do = same time as the reporting; to have done = before it; to be doing = in progress (He's thought to be hiding in Spain).1

Listen for it

Real-world task · next news clip or crime drama

Watch one news report or a police-briefing scene and catch a is believed to / is thought to line. Ask: same time or earlier? (to be or to have been?) Then, in any sitcom or chatty podcast, catch one got + past participle, such as got dumped, got caught or got fired. Bring both back to the chat.
