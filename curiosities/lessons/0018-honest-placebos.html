Honest placebos

"These are sugar pills." They helped anyway.

Time ~2 min Win a study that surprises even doctors Bonus 1 C1 word

Guess firstIn a 2010 Harvard trial, patients with irritable bowel syndrome were told plainly that their pills were placebos, like sugar pills. Compared with patients who got no pills, did their symptoms stay the same, get worse, or improve? Decide before you scroll.

Even when patients knew the pills were fake, far more of them reported relief than patients who got nothing.

59%felt adequate relief on open-label placebo
vs
35%felt adequate relief with no pills

Why it feels wrong: Ted Kaptchuk's team gave 80 IBS patients either no treatment or pills openly labelled as placebos, "like sugar pills", for three weeks; both groups got the same warm attention from staff.1 The ritual of taking pills seems to matter by itself. The caveat: it was a small pilot with self-reported symptoms, and later trials show relief of symptoms like pain, not cured disease.2

Retell it · say it out loud once
  1. Hook: "Would a sugar pill still work if the doctor told you it was a sugar pill?"
  2. Numbers: "Harvard, 2010, 80 IBS patients, three weeks: 59% felt real relief on honest placebos, 35% with no pills."
  3. Kicker: "The pill was fake, and everyone knew it. The relief was real."

scepticaladjective

Doubting that something is true or useful.3 Many patients were sceptical about pills they knew were fake, yet they still felt better.
