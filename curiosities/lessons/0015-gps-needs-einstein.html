GPS needs Einstein

Your phone's map quietly relies on relativity every single day.

Time ~2 min Win the best everyday proof of relativity Bonus 1 C1 word

Guess firstIf engineers ignored Einstein's relativity, how far off would GPS positions drift each day: ten centimetres, ten metres, or ten kilometres? Decide before you scroll.

Satellite clocks gain about 38 microseconds a day; left uncorrected, GPS errors would grow by roughly 10 km a day.

+45 µsa day faster: weaker gravity up there
−7 µsa day slower: orbital speed
+38 µsnet gain a day ≈ 11 km of light travel

Why it works: General relativity says clocks in weaker gravity tick faster; special relativity says fast-moving clocks tick slower. At 20,000 km up, gravity wins.1 GPS measures distance by light's travel time, and in 38 µs light covers about 11 km. So each satellite clock is tuned to tick slightly slow before launch.2 The "10 km a day" is a rough conversion; the real failure would be messier.

Retell it · say it out loud once
  1. Hook: "Did you know your phone's map would be useless without Einstein?"
  2. Numbers: "Weaker gravity makes satellite clocks gain 45 microseconds a day, their speed loses 7: net 38. That's about 10 km of error a day."
  3. Kicker: "Every time you follow a blue dot, you're testing relativity."

negligibleadjective

Too slight or small in amount to be of importance.3 38 microseconds sounds negligible, but at the speed of light it's about 11 kilometres.
